How to Get Your Music Reviewed: A Step-by-Step Guide for Independent Artists

Press · 9 min read

A good review does more than praise your song. It gives you a quote for your bio, a link for your EPK, and a new audience that trusts the writer's taste. Getting reviewed takes preparation and patience, but the process is straightforward when you break it down.

Choose the right reviewers

Start by reading reviews of artists in your genre. Notice which sites cover unsigned or independent music, how long their reviews are, and whether they accept submissions. A writer who covers only major label releases is unlikely to review your debut single. Aim for outlets that regularly feature emerging artists, and read a few of their recent reviews so you can speak to their style when you pitch.

Release something worth reviewing

Reviewers prefer complete works with context: a single with a video, an EP with a theme, or an album with a clear story. A solid track with professional artwork, clean audio and a working streaming link is the minimum. If your mix is rough, fix it before you pitch, because you only get one first impression.

Write a review-ready pitch

Include a one-paragraph summary of the release, what inspired it, and who it is for. Mention comparisons carefully; naming one or two similar artists helps writers place your sound, but stacking five famous names makes you look unsure of your own identity. Add links to the music, photos and your bio.

Give reviewers time

Reviewers often work weeks ahead. If an outlet accepts unreleased music, send private streaming links at least three to four weeks before release. If you are pitching an existing release, be patient: it may take a month or more to hear back, and many writers will not reply at all.

Handle the outcome gracefully

If you get a positive review, thank the writer and share it widely. If the review is mixed, stay professional. Never argue publicly with a reviewer. A balanced review still gives you exposure, and reviewers talk to each other. If you hear nothing, move on and keep the contact for your next release.

Make the most of every review

  • Pull one short quote for your bio and social posts, with credit and a link
  • Add the review to your EPK and website
  • Mention it in pitches to similar outlets

A steady stream of honest reviews builds trust more reliably than a single viral moment, so treat each one as part of a longer story.
